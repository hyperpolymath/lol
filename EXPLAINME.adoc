== Provenance of the code
 
LOL began as a rewrite of https://github.com/ehsanasgari/1000Langs[1000Langs] by Ehsaneddin Asgari, licensed Apache-2.0.
 
* *Status:* OPEN, pending a file-by-file comparison.
* *Artefact:* `docs/provenance/1000langs-comparison.adoc`, recording which parts of LOL were translated from 1000Langs and which were written independently.
* *Consequence:* derived parts carry the Apache-2.0 licence text, Asgari's copyright with the original years, and a statement of changes; independent parts carry Jonathan D.A. Jewell's copyright. Copyright headers that currently read "2024-2025 Ehsaneddin Asgari and Contributors" will be corrected either way.
 
== Why the project is designed this way
 
=== Why start from the Bible
 
It is the most widely translated text, and its chapter-and-verse numbering is an alignment key that already exists in every translation. That made it the fastest route to a skeleton covering the most languages. The choice is about coverage and alignment, not about the content.
 
=== Why an index rather than a store
 
Copying every corpus into LOL would duplicate other people's work, freeze it at the moment of copying, and make LOL responsible for terms it cannot enforce. Holding alignments, provenance and terms, and linking to sources, keeps one authoritative copy of each source where its owners maintain it.
 
=== Why disagreement is kept
 
In mathematics, conflicting results point to an error, and a system such as Echidna arbitrates between provers to reach one verdict. In language and culture, variation is often the thing being preserved: dialects, variant readings and competing translations are evidence, not noise. LOL therefore borrows the provenance and uncertainty tracking from that kind of arbitration, and drops the step that picks a winner.
 
=== Why equivalence is stated
 
Two texts in different languages are never identical; they correspond in some respect, by verse, by concept or by gloss. Saying in which respect makes every alignment a claim with a basis, which can be checked, disputed and refined. This is equivalence treated as identity up to a stated relation, rather than equality of strings.
 
=== Why provenance and consent are part of the data model
 
Preserving a community's language can slide into taking it. The CARE principles for Indigenous data governance and the Local Contexts Traditional Knowledge and Biocultural labels give communities machine-readable terms for their material. Carrying those terms with every item, and honouring them in what LOL serves, is the condition for doing preservation work at all. It is the same idea as the Palimpsest licence: provenance and terms travel with the work.
 
=== The test for adding a source
 
A source is added only if it brings at least one of:
 
* a new alignment key (a new way of saying "these texts correspond");
* new languages or communities;
* a new medium, such as speech or handwriting;
 
and comes with clear provenance and terms. This is what keeps LOL from becoming a pile of corpora.
